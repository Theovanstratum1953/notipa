Roles and how the app is scoped to your school

Every person in the app is either an admin, a teacher, or a guardian at a given school. The same person can hold different roles at different schools — for example, a staff member whose own child also attends the school will be a teacher at that school and a guardian of their child, both at once.

Everything you see (classes, students, teachers, guardians) is scoped to whichever school is currently active for you. If you belong to more than one school, a school switcher appears in the top-right of every page so you can change which one you're looking at. If you're not linked to any school yet, most sections of the app will look empty until an admin adds you.

Admins can do everything below, including adding and removing other teachers and admins. Teachers can manage Classes, Students, and Guardians, but can't add or remove other staff. Guardians see a different, much simpler experience — see What a guardian sees below — since the Students/Classes/Teachers/Guardians sections described in this page are for school staff, not guardians.

Setting up a school

Creating a brand-new school is a one-time, platform-level action, so it's restricted to superusers (the technical account created when the app was first installed) rather than regular admins — otherwise anyone could spin up an unrelated school inside the same system.

If you're a superuser: use Set Up a School under Admin in the sidebar (or the button shown on the dashboard if you're not linked to any school yet). Fill in the school's name, country, default language, timezone, tier (Free for a public school, Paid for a licensed private school), and which month its academic year starts in. Submitting the form does two things at once: it creates the school, and it makes you that school's first admin, so you land on a working dashboard immediately rather than having to set up your own access afterward.

From there, add teachers, guardians, classes, and students using the sections below. If you need a second school (a different campus, or an unrelated school entirely), the same "Set Up a School" link is always available under Admin. The Set Up a School page also lists every school already in the system, so you can check one doesn't already exist before creating a near-duplicate.

Settings

Open Settings under Admin in the sidebar (admins only) to edit your current school's own profile: its name, country code, default language, timezone, and which month its academic year starts in.

This edits whichever school is currently active for you — if you belong to more than one school, use the school switcher in the top-right first, then open Settings.

The school's tier (Free/Track 1 or Paid/Track 2) is shown here but can't be changed from this page — that's a licensing decision handled by the platform operator, not something a school changes for itself.

Guardian ↔ Teacher messaging is off by default — turn it on here once your school is ready to support real-time parent messaging (staffing, moderation, and expectations around response time are worth thinking through first). See Messaging below for what turning it on actually does.

Teachers

Open Teachers in the sidebar (admins only) to see everyone with teacher or admin access at the current school.

Adding a teacher: click New Teacher, fill in their name, choose whether they're a Teacher or an Admin, and set a username, optional email, optional phone number, and an initial password. There's no invite email or text message yet, so you'll need to pass that password to them directly — they can change it themselves later once a password-reset flow exists.

Editing a teacher: click their name in the list, then Edit Teacher. You can update their name, contact details, and switch them between Teacher and Admin. Their username can't be changed here.

Removing a teacher: there's no permanent delete. Instead, use Revoke Access on their page — this stops them from acting as a teacher/admin at this school but keeps their history (classes they taught, records they wrote) intact. If they come back, use Restore Access to reinstate them; nothing needs to be re-entered.

Guardians

Open Guardians in the sidebar (admins and teachers) to see everyone with guardian access at the current school.

Adding a guardian: click New Guardian and fill in their name, phone number (required — this is the main way a guardian is identified, even though login is currently username/password), optional email, a username, and an initial password to share with them directly, the same way as Teachers above.

Editing a guardian: click their name in the list, then Edit Guardian, to update their name and contact details.

Removing a guardian: same pattern as Teachers — Revoke Access instead of deleting, Restore Access to bring them back. Their links to students aren't affected either way.

A guardian's own page also shows every student linked to them and lets you link another — see Linking guardians and students below.

Classes

Open Classes in the sidebar to see every class/section at the current school.

Adding a class: click New Class and give it a name (e.g. "Grade 4 - Sampaguita"), an academic year (e.g. "2026-2027"), and optionally a homeroom teacher. Only teachers already added at this school show up in that dropdown — add the teacher first if they're missing (see Teachers).

Viewing a class: click its name to see its homeroom teacher, any co-teachers, and its full student roster in one place.

Editing a class: use Edit Class from either the list or the class's own page, to rename it, change its academic year, or reassign its homeroom teacher.

Removing a class: use Archive Class rather than deleting. An archived class disappears from the default list and from the Class dropdown when adding/editing students, but any students already assigned to it, and its history, are untouched. Use the Show archived link at the top of the Classes list to find it again, and Restore Class to bring it back.

Students

Open Students in the sidebar to see the full student roster at the current school.

Adding a student: click New Student and fill in their name and, optionally, date of birth, the school's own student ID, and a class. The class can always be assigned later if it's not decided yet.

Viewing a student: click their name to see their details, which class they're in, and every guardian currently linked to them.

Editing a student: use Edit Student from either the list or the student's own page.

Removing a student: use Archive Student rather than deleting. This hides them from the default roster but keeps their guardian links, records, and history intact. Use Show archived on the Students list to find them again, and Restore Student to bring them back.

Linking guardians and students

A student can have several guardians (mother, father, grandparent, or another relative/helper), and a guardian can have several students (siblings). You can create that connection from whichever side is more convenient:

From a student's page: open the student, scroll to Add a Guardian, pick from guardians already added at this school, choose the relationship (mother/father/grandparent/guardian/other), and optionally mark them as the primary contact.

From a guardian's page: open the guardian, scroll to Add a Student, and do the same in reverse — useful when you're adding several children to one guardian at once, since you only have to pick the guardian once.

Both forms create the exact same link, so it doesn't matter which one you use. Each row in either table has a Remove button, which deletes only that connection — the guardian's account and the student's own record are never affected by removing a link.

Primary contact marks who should be contacted first for a given student (for example, once SMS notifications exist). Only one guardian per student can be marked primary at a time — marking a new one automatically un-marks the previous one, so it's never ambiguous.

Editing and archiving records

Teachers, Guardians, Classes, and Students can all be edited and "removed" — but nothing in this app is ever permanently deleted through the normal UI. Instead, removing a record archives it (or, for Teachers/Guardians, revokes their access): it disappears from the everyday lists so it doesn't clutter your view, but nothing about it — its history, its links to other records — is lost, and it can always be restored later.

Classes and Students show a Show archived link at the top of their list pages so you can find and restore something that was archived by mistake. Teachers and Guardians work slightly differently: revoked people still appear in their list with a "Revoked" status, since knowing who used to have access (and being able to quickly restore it) matters more there than tidying the list.

Announcements

Open Announcements in the sidebar (admins and teachers) to see every announcement at the current school, including drafts.

Creating one: click New Announcement and write a title and body. Choose an audience — leave it as "School-wide" or pick a specific class to limit it to that class's guardians. New announcements always save as a draft first; guardians can't see it yet.

Publishing: click Publish on the list or the announcement's own page once it's ready. This is a separate, deliberate step from saving — so writing a draft and making it visible to families can never happen by accident in the same click. Unpublish reverses it, hiding the announcement from guardians again without deleting it or losing anything.

Editing: use Edit from the list, whether the announcement is a draft or already published.

Deleting: unlike Teachers/Guardians/Classes/Students, there's no archive here — Delete removes an announcement permanently, along with any guardian read receipts for it. There's a confirmation prompt before it happens.

Guardians only ever see published announcements, and only the ones relevant to them: school-wide posts, plus posts for whichever class their own linked children are in. A guardian never sees drafts, or announcements aimed at a class their child isn't in.

Homework

Open Homework in the sidebar (admins and teachers) to see every homework item at the current school.

Posting homework: click New Homework, choose the class it's for (homework is always tied to one specific class — there's no school-wide homework), a title, an optional description, an optional due date, and an optional file attachment (a worksheet, a reading, anything a guardian might need to download). Unlike Announcements, there's no draft step — homework is visible to that class's guardians as soon as you save it.

Editing: use Edit from the list to change the title, description, due date, or replace the attachment.

Deleting: like Announcements, there's no archive here — Delete removes a homework item permanently, with a confirmation prompt first.

Guardians only see homework for classes their own linked children are actually in — never other classes at the same school, even ones they can otherwise see announcements for.

Collecting submissions: turn on Accepts submissions when creating or editing a homework item to let guardians attach a file against it — a photo of completed work, a scanned worksheet, that kind of thing. It's off by default, so nothing changes for existing homework unless you switch it on.

Viewing submissions: click a homework item's title to open its detail page. If submissions are on, you'll see a roster of every active student in the class, each marked Submitted, Late, or Missing — worked out automatically from the due date and when the file came in, so there's nothing to mark by hand. "Missing" only appears once the due date has passed; before that, a student who hasn't submitted yet just shows as not yet submitted. Each row also shows who submitted it (any guardian linked to the student can) and when, with a Download link to open the file.

Fee Notices

Open Fee Notices in the sidebar (admins and teachers) to see every fee notice at the current school. This is informational only — no payments are processed in the app; it's a way to let a family know a fee is due and track whether it's been settled.

Posting a fee notice: click New Fee Notice, pick the student it applies to (a fee notice always belongs to one specific student, not a whole class or the whole school), a title (e.g. "Term 2 tuition"), an optional description, the amount and currency, and a due date. It's visible to that student's guardians as soon as you save it.

Marking a fee as paid or waived: use the Mark Paid or Waive button on the Fee Notices list. This is a separate action from editing, on purpose — changing the amount or due date and marking something as settled are different enough actions that keeping them apart avoids accidentally flipping the status while fixing a typo. Use Mark Unpaid to reverse either one if it was set by mistake.

Editing: use Edit from the list to change the student, title, description, amount, currency, or due date. The status itself isn't changed from the edit form — use the Mark Paid/Waive/Mark Unpaid buttons instead.

Deleting: like Announcements and Homework, there's no archive here — Delete removes a fee notice permanently, with a confirmation prompt first.

A guardian only ever sees fee notices for their own linked children, never another family's.

Permission Slips

Open Permission Slips in the sidebar (admins and teachers) to see every permission slip at the current school, along with how many guardians have said yes, said no, or haven't responded yet.

Posting a permission slip: click New Permission Slip and fill in a title (e.g. "Museum field trip"), an optional description, an audience — "School-wide" or a specific class — and optional event and response-deadline dates. Saving it automatically sets up a response to track for every active student in that audience who has at least one guardian linked — nothing further to set up.

Tracking responses: click a permission slip's title (or View) to see every student's response, who responded, and when — a real roster, not just a count. Editing a slip later (e.g. widening it to more classes) automatically adds tracking for any newly-eligible students without disturbing responses that already came in.

Editing: use Edit from the list to change the title, description, audience, or dates.

Deleting: like Announcements, Homework, and Fee Notices, there's no archive here — Delete removes a permission slip permanently, along with its response records, with a confirmation prompt first.

A guardian sees a permission slip for each of their own children it applies to, and can respond Yes or No with an optional note — they can change their answer any time by submitting again. A guardian only ever sees and responds for their own linked children, never another family's.

Calendar

Open Calendar in the sidebar to see a school's closed days — public holidays, in-service days, and school-declared breaks. It's a calendar of exceptions, not a full scheduling system: no timetables, no period-by-period class schedules, just which days the school is open or not.

Managing closed days (admins only): click New Closed Day and give it a label (e.g. "Independence Day" or "Term 1 break"), a start date, an end date, and a type (public holiday, in-service day, or other). A single-day closure just sets the start and end date the same; a longer break (a two-week holiday, say) is one entry with a start and end date rather than one row per day. Use Edit or Delete from the list to change or remove one — deleting has a confirmation prompt and, like Announcements/Homework/Fee Notices/Permission Slips, there's no archive here.

Unlike most other sections in this app, this one is deliberately admin-only to manage — teachers get the same read-only view guardians get, rather than write access, so there's one person per school actively curating it rather than several editing it independently.

Due-date warnings: the due-date fields on Homework, Fee Notices, and Permission Slips check against this same calendar. If you pick a date that falls on a closed day, a warning appears under the field — but it's just that, a warning: the app won't stop you from saving it, since schools sometimes do want a due date to land right after a break on purpose.

Teachers and guardians see a read-only version of this page listing upcoming closed days only — past ones drop off automatically rather than cluttering the list, though admins still see the full history on their management view.

Attendance

Open Attendance in the sidebar (admins and teachers) for a simple daily roster: present, absent, or late, taken once a day per class. It's deliberately not a period-by-period tracker — Notipa has no timetable concept, and this doesn't introduce one.

Taking the roster: pick a class from the Attendance list (or click Take Attendance from the class's own page) and every active student defaults to Present. Mark anyone absent or late and save — it's a fast checklist, not a form per student. A class's page and the Attendance list both show a Not yet taken tag for today until someone does this, so nobody has to rely on memory to know whether it's been done.

Retaking a day: reopening the same date and saving again updates that day's records in place — there's no way to end up with two conflicting entries for the same student on the same day.

Looking at other days: use the previous/next-day links on the roster page. You can't take attendance for a date that hasn't happened yet.

Editing older entries: today's roster is always editable by any teacher or admin. Anything older needs an admin to change it — a same-day correction (a student arrived late and was marked absent by mistake) is unrestricted, but the historical record beyond that isn't casually rewritten after the fact.

Closed days: if a date falls on something already in the school Calendar, the roster shows a note that it's a closed day — same as the due-date warnings elsewhere, it's informational, not a block, in case a school genuinely needs to record something for that day.

School Overview (admins only): a School Overview link shows present/absent/late totals per class over roughly the last 30 days — for spotting a pattern of absences without having to open every class's roster individually.

A guardian only ever sees their own child's attendance history, never the rest of the class's — see What a guardian sees below.

Terms

Open Terms under Admin in the sidebar (admins only) to define the school's own academic terms — Term 1, Term 2, Term 3, or however your school breaks up its year. This is a one-time-per-year setup step: give each term a name and a start/end date.

Terms exist mainly to organize Report Cards — each report card belongs to exactly one term — and a term's date range is also what a report card's attendance summary is calculated over. A term's name has to be unique at your school, and its end date can't be before its start date.

Teachers and guardians never manage terms directly; a term only shows up for them as a dropdown when working with report cards.

Report Cards

Open Report Cards in the sidebar (admins and teachers) to replace the photocopied slip sent home in a backpack with a term-by-term academic report per student, delivered straight to their guardians through Notipa. This isn't a gradebook — no weighted assessments, no standards tracking, no transcripts — just a single, dated document per student per term: subject grades, an attendance summary, and a comment.

Getting started: pick a class, then a term (use Terms first if none exist yet) to see every active student's report status — Not started, Draft, or Published. Teachers only see classes they actually teach; an admin can enter a report for any class, on a teacher's behalf.

Filling in a report: click Start (or Edit) on a student to add subject/grade rows and an optional comment. Grades are free text — a letter grade, a percentage, a descriptive mark, whatever your school actually uses. Use + Add Subject Row to add more rows on the fly, and Remove on any row you don't need — both happen instantly, without saving the page first. The first time you start a report for a class/term where a sibling student's report already has subjects entered, those subject names are copied in automatically (with the grade left blank) so you're not retyping the same subject list for every student.

An Attendance This Term box on the report shows that student's present/absent/late counts, pulled in automatically from Attendance for the term's date range — nothing to total up by hand.

Draft vs. published: the same pattern as Announcements — Save Draft keeps a report invisible to guardians so you can come back to it later; Publish makes it visible. Once a class is finished, use Publish All Drafts on the roster page to publish every draft for that class/term in one action, rather than one by one.

A guardian sees every published report for their own child, by term, newest first, and can open a clean, print-friendly view (no sidebar, no app menu) to save or print a copy — see What a guardian sees below. A guardian never sees a draft, and never sees another family's child's report.

Messaging

Direct, private, one-to-one messages between a guardian and their child's teacher — the "quick question" channel Announcements and Homework aren't built for, since those only ever go one way. Off by default for every school; an admin turns it on from Settings when the school is actually ready to support it — see Turning messaging on and off below for exactly what that commits the school to. Turning it on also switches on class group threads below, which share the same on/off switch.

Starting a conversation: either side can start one. A teacher clicks Message next to a guardian's name on that student's page (Students → the student → Guardians). A guardian clicks Message [teacher's name] on their child's page, under the class/homeroom-teacher card. Either way, it opens (or reopens, if one already exists) the same single thread for that guardian-teacher-student combination.

Who you can message: a one-to-one thread only exists where a real connection already exists in the data — a guardian can only message a teacher who actually teaches their child's class (homeroom or a co-teacher), and a teacher can only message a guardian actually linked to that student. There's no open staff directory; if a guardian has two children with two different teachers, that's two separate, unrelated threads.

The conversation itself: plain text messages with timestamps, listed oldest to newest, with a composer at the bottom — file attachments aren't part of this first version. Your own sent messages show Sent until the other person actually opens the thread, then switch to Seen, the same read-receipt idea Announcements already uses.

Messages in the sidebar (guardians and teachers only, and only once a school has turned messaging on) lists every conversation you're part of — one-to-one and class group threads together — most recently active first, with an unread count on any thread that has something new.

Message content isn't automatically moderated by Notipa itself — a school turning this on takes on the same responsibility it already has for any other parent-teacher channel. (Class group threads do have a teacher-moderated Remove action — see below.)

Class Group Messaging

A shared thread for a whole class — its teacher and every one of its students' guardians in one place — the "class WhatsApp group," but inside Notipa, moderated by the teacher, and scoped only to that class. It shares the same underlying model as one-to-one Messaging above and the same per-school on/off switch, so there's nothing separate to turn on at the school level.

Automatic, no invites: a class gets its own group thread the moment it has at least one enrolled student and messaging is on for it (school switch on, and the class itself hasn't opted out — see below). From there, membership takes care of itself — a guardian is added the moment their child is enrolled in the class, and removed the moment their child leaves it or the guardian link is removed; a teacher is added or removed as they're assigned as homeroom or a co-teacher. Nobody has to send an invite, and nobody can be added by hand.

Getting to it: a teacher opens a class's group thread from that class's page (Classes → the class → Class Group Chat), if they're actually teaching it. A guardian opens it from their child's page, the same place the one-to-one Message [teacher] buttons live.

Who's in it: the thread page lists every current participant, so it's never an anonymous crowd — you can always see who else is in the conversation.

Teacher moderation: a class's teacher(s) can remove a message. Notipa doesn't do automated content moderation, so the teacher is the backstop, the same as in a real classroom group. A removed message doesn't disappear outright — it's replaced with a visible "message removed" placeholder, so the rest of the conversation doesn't lose context.

Announcements only: a teacher can flip a class thread to announcements-only from the thread page, if the class's dynamics call for it — guardians can still read everything, but only the teacher can post until it's switched back.

Like one-to-one Messaging, there are no file attachments and no per-message read receipts in this first version — just plain text with an unread badge on the thread.

Turning messaging on and off

Messaging is a real operational commitment for a school — someone has to be expected to actually read and respond — so it's opt-in at two levels, not just one.

School-level (admins): off by default on a fresh install. Settings → the messaging checkbox turns on both one-to-one guardian ↔ teacher messaging and class group messaging for the whole school in one step.

Class-level (teachers and admins): once the school switch is on, every class defaults to messaging on too — it's opt-out at the class level, not opt-in, so nobody has to remember to turn each class on individually. A teacher who'd rather not run a class group chat for their own class can uncheck "Messaging for this class" from that class's own edit page (Classes → the class → Edit Class) — that field only appears there once the school switch is on; there's nothing to configure per class while the school itself has messaging off, so it stays hidden until it would actually mean something.

Clean disable, not deletion: turning either switch off hides the "start a conversation" buttons (Message [teacher], Class Group Chat) and blocks new threads and new messages at the permission layer, not just in the screen you're looking at. It does not delete anything — every existing thread and message stays exactly where it was, still reachable from Messages in the sidebar, just read-only until the switch is back on. Turning a switch back on doesn't recreate anything either; the same threads simply become postable again.

Installing the app and turning on notifications

Open App & Notifications under Help in the sidebar — the same page for every role, since installing and enabling notifications is a per-device choice, not a school setting.

Installing: on Android or a desktop browser like Chrome or Edge, click Install Notipa to add it as a real app — full screen, its own icon, no address bar. On an iPhone or iPad, there's no one-click button (Safari doesn't offer one); instead, tap the Share button and choose Add to Home Screen.

Notifications: click Enable Notifications and accept your browser's permission prompt to get a real alert on this device — even with Notipa closed — the moment something new is published: an announcement, homework, a fee notice, a permission slip, a report card, or a new message. On iPhone, this only works once Notipa is installed to your home screen first (iOS 16.4 or later) — opening it from a browser tab isn't enough for push notifications there, an Apple platform limitation, not a Notipa one. Click Turn Off on This Device any time to stop; it only affects the device you're using; other devices (or other people) you've enabled notifications on keep working.

If your school hasn't set up push notifications on its server yet, this page says so plainly rather than showing a button that wouldn't do anything — nothing else in the app depends on this feature, so there's no rush to configure it.

What a guardian sees

Everything above except Announcements, Homework, Fee Notices, Permission Slips, Calendar, Attendance, Report Cards, and Messaging is for school staff. A guardian account sees something much simpler when they log in: their dashboard shows the child (or children) linked to their account at the current school, with each one's class and homeroom teacher. Clicking a child opens a read-only page with the same class/teacher information, plus that child's recent announcements, recent homework, recent fee notices, recent permission slip status, recent attendance, and recent report cards — a guardian can't edit a student's details, archive anyone, or add/remove guardian links from their own account.

A guardian's own Announcements page (same sidebar link, different content than staff see) lists every published, relevant announcement with a Mark as read button on each unread one.

A guardian's own Homework page lists every homework item posted for their children's classes, with the due date, description, and a download link for any attachment. If a homework item accepts submissions, an upload control appears underneath it for each of your children in that class — attach a file (an image or a PDF, size-capped) and an optional note. You can replace it any time before the due date passes; after that, a first-ever submission is still accepted and simply flagged Late, but a submission that's already there can no longer be replaced. If more than one guardian is linked to the same child, any of you can submit or replace it, and all of you see the same up-to-date status.

A guardian's own Fee Notices page lists every fee notice for their own children, with the amount, due date, description, and whether it's currently unpaid, paid, or waived. Like everywhere else in the app, no payments are taken here — it's informational only.

A guardian's own Permission Slips page lists every permission slip for their own children, with a Yes/No button and an optional notes field for each one still awaiting a response — and shows their existing answer for ones they've already responded to, which they can change any time by submitting again.

A guardian's own Calendar page lists the school's upcoming closed days, same as what teachers see — read-only, so you can tell at a glance why school's out next Thursday.

From your child's own page, a Recent Attendance card shows their latest present/absent/late records with a View all link to the full history — always just that one child's, never the rest of their class.

A Recent Report Cards card on the same page lists their published reports, with a View all link to see every term. Each one opens as a clean, print-friendly page — no sidebar or menu — so you can save or print a copy. You'll only ever see a report once a teacher has published it; a report still being worked on doesn't show up here at all. If more than one guardian is linked to the same child, you both see the exact same published reports and attendance history.

If your school has turned on messaging, a Message [teacher] button appears on your child's page for each teacher actually teaching their class. Sending a first message starts a private thread with just that teacher, about just that child — Messages in the sidebar lists all of your conversations, and other guardians linked to the same child don't see or share your threads with a teacher; each guardian's conversations are their own. A Class Group Chat button appears alongside it, which opens the whole class's shared thread with its teacher and every other guardian in the class — see Class Group Messaging.

If a guardian logs in and sees no children listed, it means nobody has linked their account to a student yet — a school admin or teacher needs to do that from the student's or guardian's own page (see Linking guardians and students).
